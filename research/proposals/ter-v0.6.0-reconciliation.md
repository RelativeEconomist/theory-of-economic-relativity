# TER v0.6.0 architectural reconciliation

**Recommendation.** Keep the candidate’s four groups of obligations, with persistence identified as a derived consistency check. Use the independent review’s concrete tests for admissibility, information, and settlement. Require dynamic specifications to declare what agents receive and how objective and agent-side states change. Require independently stated economic constraints where they apply. A feasibility correspondence, a universal equilibrium operator, and a smaller list of named TER objects are unnecessary for these changes. ## 1. Four clauses and six restrictions

| Independent restriction | Reconciliation with the four-clause candidate |
| --- | --- |
| **T1. Agent-side selection** | Keep under **Selection**. Use a structural input exclusion. Include optional valuation and the specified decision rule and draw. The proposed conditional independence given only $M,\hat F$ is neither necessary nor sufficient for the exclusion. |
| **T2. Perceived support** | Keep under **Selection** and state it once. Require $C\in\hat F$, or probability one on the measurable menu at each admitted stochastic choice context. Topological support can include the endpoints of an open menu even when the choice lands inside it almost surely. |
| **T3. Realization inputs** | Keep under **Objective realization**. At fixed selected actions and realization conditions, varying an agent-side belief leaves the realization fixed. Beliefs may still change settlement through decisions and other modeled causal channels. |
| **T4. Constrained realization** | Add an explicit specification check under **Objective realization**. Verify the applicable constraints against the outputs of $R$. An independent correspondence is one way to express the check. |
| **T5. Joint consistency** | Keep under **Objective realization**. Test individual and system readouts against one realization. An individual readout need only be recoverable from the common joint result. The reported system outcome can be too coarse to recover it. |
| **T6. Observation-mediated updating** | Keep under **Feedback**. Use separately timed pre-choice signals and post-event observations where relevant. Retain the objective transition separately, as the independent review’s Def-Φ does. Preserve local memory, own records, and declared internal updates. |

**Redundancy.** Canonical A2’s membership clause follows from A4. A3 supplies the meaning of valuation when used. A6 and A9 permit later effects, including effects on other agents. They imply no nonzero response, since a permitted causal pathway can be inactive. A8 follows once the admissible continuation set is specified and is useful as a check on that specification. Its retention as a fourth clause conveys its role without treating it as an independent economic law. [Canonical axioms](../../theory/academic.md#4-axioms) · [Six restrictions and definitions](ter-independent-adversarial-review.md#91-replace-the-nine-axioms-with-six-restrictions-and-three-definitions)

**Independence on a weak signature.** Information access and menu membership exclude different models. A chooser reading a hidden bit can stay inside its menu, while a constant chooser can select outside it. A valid chooser can be followed by settlement that reads a private belief at fixed actions and reality. A belief-free settlement can overpay. A balanced joint settlement can be accompanied by an inconsistent individual report, such as naming the wrong recipient while preserving the total. Finally, all within-event checks can pass while a later belief update uses a withheld receipt. These witnesses separate the obligations when the types allow each violation. Once a single joint result and its readouts are part of the signature, T5’s common-origin requirement follows by construction. The double-payment example alone cannot establish T5’s independence from a resource constraint. T6 also leaves a separate objective-transition exclusion to state.

**Why T1 is structural.** Hold $M$ and $\hat F=\{0,1\}$ constant. An objective $G$ can be correlated with the realized state $F$ through a common cause. If $V(a\mid G)=\mathbf 1\{a=G\}$ and $G=F$ on the observed sample, a valid maximizing decision has $C=F$. Conversely, a forbidden rule $C=F\oplus U$ with an independent fair draw $U$ has $C\perp F$ while reading $F$ at fixed $U$. Test allowed inputs and controlled variations. For T3, deterministic $R$ gives the corresponding conditional independence when conditioned on the complete $C,F_t$. Empirical independence tests require additional measurement and dependence assumptions.

**Reading conflicts.** Essential dependence on beliefs in A1 would contradict the explicit allowance for habits in §5.1. State the allowed input channels and permit constant rules. An unconditional reading of the reality definition would contradict belief-induced actions. A7 should be read at fixed actions and objective realization conditions, so the two healthy-bank equilibria do not contradict it. A5’s action contribution identifies a channel. A nonzero action effect requires an outcome and an admissible contrast. [Canonical decision and realization models](../../theory/academic.md#51-agent-decision-model)

## 2. Feasibility, observation, and transition

**Feasibility has economic content.** The type of $R$ cannot enforce a budget. The independent review’s rule paying three tenders $7/5$ each against a cap of $14/5$ is well typed and breaches the independently stated cap by $7/5$. This exposes an underspecified formal check. Once the cap is part of $F_t$, the rule is economically inadmissible under v0.5.0’s realization boundary. Require a predicate $K(c,f,y)$ on the declared input and outcome domains wherever the model asserts an invariant. Check $K(c,f,R(c,f))$ for every admitted input, including failed and partially executed attempts.

An optional $\Gamma(c,f)=\{y:K(c,f,y)\}$ carries the same restriction. A state-only $\Gamma(f)$ suffices for a state-only resource bound. It can miss action-dependent constraints. With one unit and a payment-only outcome, both zero and one can be feasible at the same $f$, yet paying one on a no-order profile is invalid. Use a correspondence conditional on the action profile, a set of admissible action–outcome pairs, or the predicate directly. Defining $\Gamma$ as the range of the proposed $R$ makes the check circular. Changing the type of $F_t$ to $(F_t,\Gamma)$ adds no protection. Success of one attempt may depend on the other actions and service priority. $\hat F$ continues to describe what the agent perceives as available to attempt. [Canonical feasibility](../../theory/academic.md#objective-feasible-state-of-reality) · [Independent feasibility challenge](ter-independent-adversarial-review.md#53-feasibility-has-no-formal-content)

**Maps are necessary content for a complete dynamic model.** Different disclosure or transition rules can produce different paths with the same $D$ and $R$. Declare these mechanisms as functions or kernels where active, with initial conditions and any joint randomness. An observation can be a readout of a recorded release. A joint kernel can carry both next state and deliveries if their timing and intervention responses remain recoverable. Constant carry-forward or an empty report can cover an inactive channel. A pre-choice channel defined mechanically on the later realization snapshot can reveal an unresolved queue draw. Use dated inputs and permit selection to see only the delivered record and legitimate local state. A transition solely on $F_t$ is adequate only if $F_t$ retains everything relevant to the successor. Keep the required objective history or stocks in the specification’s system state. ## 3. Counterexample tests

### Psychological games

Simple guilt concerns disappointing the other player relative to that player’s expectation of **their own** material payoff. The independent review’s displayed expression uses the wrong payoff index. In the source model, decision utility uses the actor’s first- and higher-order beliefs, and the belief-dependent payoff need not be an experienced outcome. The source’s simple-guilt construction also accounts for how much disappointment the actor could have avoided. [Battigalli and Dufwenberg, *Guilt in Games*](https://www.aeaweb.org/annual_mtg_papers/2007/0105_1430_0803.pdf)

For an exact two-action witness, let Grab yield material payoffs $(1,0)$ and Share yield $(0,2)$. Let $b\in[0,2]$ be the other player’s initial expected receipt and set guilt sensitivity to one. Sharing can avoid all disappointment. At fixed Grab, the adjusted payoff $1-b$ is $1$ at $b=0$ and $-1$ at $b=2$, with identical material settlement. The source incentives can be represented by evaluating the same payoff functional under the actor’s beliefs about $b$, with actual belief-dependent game payoffs reported as separate derived evaluations. This keeps the actor’s assessment distinct from the other player’s actual belief. If the research target instead requires an immediate realized outcome to vary with the other player’s privately held belief at fixed $C,F_t$, then $R(C,F_t)$ cannot preserve that target. A disclosed expectation or a belief report that enters a contract is a different, observable causal channel. **Disposition.** Compatible as a valuation and solution concept. A genuine scope limit for the stronger private-belief realization claim.

### Delayed realization

Accept a buy limit order for one unit at price ten. The acceptance and standing order are recorded at the first event. A later sell order at nine fills it under the declared matching rule, while an ask at eleven leaves it open. The buyer makes one choice. The later joint settlement uses the carried instruction and later order, and records a fill linked to the original order. Investment and future income use the same staging principle with installed capital and later production or sales. Replaying the later match at recorded conditions answers a controlled realization question. Changing the earlier instruction requires a rerun from the earlier event, with a declared treatment of later information and choices. Sequential service follows the same rule. Carry the opening balance in $F_t$, and calculate intermediate balances within joint $R$ when there are no intervening decisions. Use later stages if agents receive information or can act between payments. **Disposition.** Compatible, with an explicit timing and attribution specification. [Commitments and feedback in v0.5.0](https://github.com/RelativeEconomist/theory-of-economic-relativity/blob/cc9547d231ba29adbc3c834049af094c0db8a780/theory/academic.md#55-dynamic-feedback-model) · [Bank-run results](../experiments/bank_run_identification/comparison.md)

### Simultaneous rational expectations

An informative-price equilibrium fixes a price function $p=P(s)$ of the signal profile $s$. Where the conditional law is defined, agents’ equilibrium beliefs agree with the source model’s law given $(s_i,P(s))$. Given those beliefs and the price, their decisions meet the source optimality conditions and clear the market. These restrictions can be checked jointly. Submitted contingent schedules can also be cleared by $R$ when the source mechanism permits them as choices. Solving the equations establishes consistency. A real-time price-discovery claim requires a mechanism. [Grossman, 1976, equation 12](https://e-m-h.org/Gros76.pdf)

An exact illustration uses independent fair bits $s_1,s_2$ and $\theta=(s_1+s_2)/2$. At the candidate price $p=\theta$, the posterior mean $m_i=\mathbb E[\theta\mid s_i,p]$ equals $p$. The strictly concave payoff $(s_i+m_i-2p)q_i-q_i^2/2$ gives net demand $q_i=s_i-p$. It clears in all four signal profiles. For an executable schedule, extend the off-support mean by clipping $p$ to $[s_i/2,(s_i+1)/2]$. The sum of the two demands is strictly decreasing in $p$ and has the same root. Uniqueness here is conditional on those schedules.

The earlier candidate’s $q=p,\ p=2-q$ example tests a stronger claim. Its unique fixed point is $(1,1)$, but forcing $p=1/2$ implies $q=1/2$ under literal simultaneous equations. A TER stage with $q$ chosen before $p$ is realized cannot give that response with unchanged choice inputs. An earlier price message or a contingent instruction changes the intervention being modeled. **Disposition.** Existing mechanisms express equilibrium consistency. A literal action–outcome cycle with both same-event interventions preserved needs separate theory.

### Continuum economies

Read the finite display in §5.3 as a finite-profile case. For an atomless population $([0,1],\nu)$ with Lebesgue measure, take measurable $c(i)\in\{0,1\}$ and aggregate demand $\int c(i)\,d\nu(i)$. A unilateral change leaves the integral fixed. A finite weighted sum with strictly positive individual weights changes for the same deviation. Finite models can assume price taking, but that assumption does not reproduce the atomless aggregation property. A specification may use a measurable profile domain and a joint $R$ on that domain. Replacing the profile by its distribution requires an anonymity or sufficiency argument. If individual readouts are required at particular agents, equivalence classes modulo null sets may be too coarse. Stochastic continuum examples additionally need joint measurability and justified integration. **Disposition.** The finite formula needs a stated general domain for an exact continuum claim. A deterministic integral example works without changing the economic boundaries. A general stochastic extension needs its own proof. [Aumann, 1964](https://www.ma.huji.ac.il/raumann/pdf/Markets%20with%20a%20Continuum%20of%20Traders.pdf) · [Sun, 2006](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022053104002224)

## 4. Minimal candidate architecture

The following are proposed specification interfaces. Declare spaces $\mathcal S_t$ for opening objective states $S_t$, $\mathcal F_t$ for realization snapshots $F_t$, and $\mathcal B_{i,t}^{\pm}$ for agent-side component tuples $B_{i,t}^{\pm}$ at choice and after updates. Declare the event records $\mathcal H_{\le d}$ available to a delivery mechanism at time $d$ and its signal spaces $\mathcal L_{i,t}^{\pm}$. The delivery maps return signals in those spaces, the update maps return tuples in the declared agent-state spaces, and $T_t$ returns a state in $\mathcal S_{t+1}$. Declare initial states, the acting set, allowed input tuples, the ordering of events and any joint law of draws. For a stochastic specification, specify measurable spaces and admissible maps or kernels. For a finite model these requirements reduce to ordinary tables and functions.

1. **Selection.** For each acting agent, declare an action space $\mathcal A_i$ and a nonempty perceived menu $\hat F_{i,t}\subseteq\mathcal A_i$. Before selection, use a specified delivery rule and agent update,

   $$
   y^-_{i,t}=\sigma^-_{i,t}(\mathcal H_{\le d^-_{i,t}}),\qquad
   B^-_{i,t}=U^-_{i,t}(B^+_{i,t-1},y^-_{i,t}),\qquad
   C_{i,t}=D_{i,t}(\hat F_{i,t},M_{i,t},v_{i,t};u^D_{i,t})\in\hat F_{i,t}.
   $$

   An agent with no new information can carry its state forward. On admitted inputs, $D_{i,t}$ returns an element of $\mathcal A_i$ using components from $B^-_{i,t}$ and any permitted choice draw. If valuation is used, $v_{i,t}=V_{i,t}(\cdot\mid G_{i,t},M_{i,t},H_{i,t})$ has the domain and score space specified for that rule. Otherwise omit the argument. Keep $G,H$ as named roles, with fixed values bound into $V$ where appropriate. The optional optimization example should use $\arg\max V$ when $V$ is already an expected score. If it is state-contingent, name the subjective probability law and integrability conditions. In either case, specify existence of maximizing choices and how any ties are resolved. Requiring expected utility for all valuations would narrow v0.5.0. Menu context may reach valuation through declared information about alternatives, with $\hat F$ retaining its role in selection. A universal menu argument to $V$ requires separate justification.

2. **Objective realization.** Form $F_t=\Phi_t(S_t,\xi_t)$ from the relevant objective state and any additional conditions resolved by realization. Declare a joint action-profile domain $\mathcal C_t$ and an admitted input domain $\mathcal D_t\subseteq\mathcal C_t\times\mathcal F_t$. A finite profile uses the acting set’s action spaces. The domain covers the recorded attempts, including those that fail. Let $R_t:\mathcal D_t\to\mathcal Y_t$ return one joint record $Y_t$ with declared readouts.

   $$
   Y_t=R_t(\mathbf C_t,F_t),\quad O_t=P_{\mathrm{sys}}(Y_t),\quad
   O_{i,t}=P_i(Y_t)\ \text{where defined},\quad K(\mathbf C_t,F_t,Y_t)\ \text{where required}.
   $$

   The readouts are specification notation. A separate Model 5.2 response is adequate for an individual readout exactly when it is invariant to variations of companion actions at fixed $F_t,C_{i,t}$ on the query domain. Sufficiency follows by defining the response from any representative of each nonempty fiber. Necessity follows because the two inputs of the separate response are fixed. Include admitted counterfactual profiles in the domain when claiming replay. For fixed-state replay, reproduce the factual $Y_t$ before substituting a selected action. Retain the resolved draw or response coupling for stochastic replay.

3. **Feedback.** Declare the objective transition and the information available to each agent before its next decision,

   $$
   S_{t+1}=T_t(S_t,F_t,Y_t;\varepsilon_{t+1}),\qquad
   y^+_{i,t}=\sigma^+_{i,t}(\mathcal H_{\le d^+_{i,t}}),\qquad
   B^+_{i,t}=U^+_{i,t}(B^-_{i,t},y^+_{i,t}).
   $$

   Each delivery uses events already available to its channel. Where draws are needed, declare them and their timing. Objective changes caused by modeled actions pass through consequences in $Y_t$. An enforceable commitment can enter those consequences. Preserve the independently defined observation boundary on beliefs. Specify the process for outside shocks and local internal updates. An outside shock can be statistically associated with actions. State the realized event that transmits each modeled action effect. A copy of the action in a log gives no mechanism for the transition.

4. **Persistence check.** Let $\operatorname{CanContinue}(S_t,F_t)$ denote whether continuation of the relevant state is possible under its independently specified conditions. If it is false, an unchanged successor is inadmissible. Specify a horizon, stochastic quantifier, and class of admissible behavior before using a viability kernel. Viability is a useful analysis for particular models. Defining imbalance as viability under some policy combined with failure under the current policy would change the canonical concept, which allows pressure and continued persistence together.

## 6. Proof obligations and regression requirements

**Conservative mapping.** For the finite staged class, map each fully specified v0.5.0 model that satisfies its declared objective constraints and timing into these interfaces. Map back by reading the observation, update, and transition functions as specification-level rules. When the initial law and each dated conditional mechanism agree, induction gives the same trace law. An intervention or replay also requires the same substituted mechanism and any necessary shared draws. This argument establishes conditional trace equivalence. A broader equivalence claim requires proof for its domain, including any effects of strengthening feasibility declarations. Equal forecast laws explain the bank-run score tie. The result does not rank either architecture’s research value.

**Required checks before adoption.**

| Area | Regression requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Selection and information | Admit a constant habit and stochastic choices in an open menu. Reject hidden-state access even when marginal independence holds. Vary an early notice and withhold a post-event receipt. Verify that late queue information never enters the earlier choice. |
| Realization and constraints | Reproduce the 648 exact bank histories, equilibrium sets, priority, and replay. Reject the overpaying rule and a balanced but inconsistent individual report. Check actual and believed prices separately. Verify that identical payout marginals can give different same-draw replays. |
| Deferred settlement | Preserve the original limit order, allow a fill at nine and reject a fill at eleven under the example rule. Confirm fixed-state replay and an earlier-order intervention answer their respective questions. |
| Psychological valuation | Preserve material receipts and the two distinct psychological valuations in the Grab witness. Verify subjective higher-order beliefs can enter choice without supplying the other agent’s actual private belief to $R$. |
| Equilibrium | Verify all four informative-price signal profiles, belief consistency on supported prices, and clearing. Exercise empty and multiple solution sets. Preserve the existing supply-and-demand and Krugman comparisons. Require a separately specified adjustment model for convergence claims. |
| Population domain | Verify the deterministic atomless aggregate is invariant to a singleton deviation. Before claiming stochastic continuum support, prove the requisite measurability and aggregation properties for the chosen construction. |

The two reviews used different notation for many of the same obligations. Adoption should depend on their conservative mapping and the checks above. Empirical prediction and reviewer auditability remain separate research questions.
