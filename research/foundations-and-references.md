# Foundations and References

The Theory of Economic Relativity is built on established economics, not apart from it. This file documents the established research TER draws on, shows how each mechanism maps into TER's architecture, and gives researchers durable primary references rather than secondary summaries.

TER does not claim concepts such as scarcity, constrained choice, bounded rationality, asymmetric information, strategic interaction, externalities, institutions, equilibrium, behavioral economics, heterogeneous agents, or economic dynamics as novel. Its contribution is the common architecture used to organize and connect them: many of these established mechanisms may be represented as configurations or processes within a common agent-centered architecture, without changing what they mean (see [§7.7](../theory/academic.md#77-established-economic-theories-may-be-represented-within-a-common-framework) and the [Central Research Claim](../theory/academic.md#central-research-claim)).

This document is contextual and bibliographic. It does not override, extend, or reinterpret `theory/academic.md` — where a mapping below could be read as adding a new TER claim, it has been narrowed instead. For canonical definitions, axioms, formal architecture, claims, and limitations, see [`theory/academic.md`](../theory/academic.md).

## Established Concepts and TER Mappings

| Established concept | TER representation | TER reference | Key references |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scarcity and constrained choice | `F_t` and Constraints define what is actually available; choosing among competing alternatives creates opportunity costs — opportunity cost is not a separate TER primitive. | [Constraints](../theory/academic.md#constraints) | Robbins (1932); Samuelson (1947) |
| Bounded rationality | Limited or simplified `M`, a narrower `F̂` than what `F_t` permits, and a non-optimizing `D` (satisficing, heuristics). | [§7.7](../theory/academic.md#77-established-economic-theories-may-be-represented-within-a-common-framework); [Decision Process](../theory/academic.md#decision-process) | Simon (1955) |
| Asymmetric information | Differences in `M` across agents about economically relevant conditions; those differences may also affect subsequent perceptions and decisions. | [§7.7](../theory/academic.md#77-established-economic-theories-may-be-represented-within-a-common-framework); [Model of Reality](../theory/academic.md#model-of-reality) | Akerlof (1970); Spence (1973); Rothschild & Stiglitz (1976) |
| Strategic interaction | Interdependent agent decisions and outcomes; TER does not assume a particular solution concept — that choice belongs to the specification's `D`. | [§7.7](../theory/academic.md#77-established-economic-theories-may-be-represented-within-a-common-framework); [§5.3 Multi Agent Model](../theory/academic.md#53-multi-agent-model) | von Neumann & Morgenstern (1944); Nash (1951) |
| Externalities | A consequence experienced by other agents that was **not fully considered** in the originating agent's decision — not every spillover qualifies automatically. | [External Effect](../theory/academic.md#external-effect) | Pigou (1920); Coase (1960) |
| Institutions | May alter `M`, `F̂`, `V`, and `D` (information, perceived feasible actions, incentives, decision processes), with their laws and rules forming part of `F_t`, and may themselves act as agents. | [§7.7](../theory/academic.md#77-established-economic-theories-may-be-represented-within-a-common-framework); [Institution](../theory/academic.md#institution) | North (1990) |
| General equilibrium | Interdependent agent decisions and system outcomes can represent the interactions studied by general equilibrium theory. TER does not require markets to clear or assume that an equilibrium exists, is unique, or is optimal. | [§5.3 Multi Agent Model](../theory/academic.md#53-multi-agent-model); [Equilibrium](../theory/academic.md#equilibrium) | Walras (1954 [1874]); Arrow & Debreu (1954) |
| Behavioral economics | Heuristics, biases, and reference-dependent valuation live in `M`, `V`, `D` — TER never assumes optimization or full rationality. | [§5.1 Agent Decision Model](../theory/academic.md#51-agent-decision-model) | Kahneman & Tversky (1979) |
| Heterogeneous agents | Each agent's `G`, `M`, `F̂`, `V`, `H`, `D` is independently specified per agent — no two agents are required to share values or a decision process. | [TER Variables and Notation](../theory/academic.md#ter-variables-and-notation) | Aiyagari (1994); Krusell & Smith (1998) |
| Dynamics and stability | Feedback architecture (Dynamic Feedback Model, Feedback Analysis and Stability) plus optional, specification-specific analytical methods (state-space, Jacobians, eigenvalues, simulation) — these analytical objects are not TER primitives. | [§5.5 Dynamic Feedback Model](../theory/academic.md#55-dynamic-feedback-model); [§5.6 Feedback Analysis and Stability](../theory/academic.md#56-feedback-analysis-and-stability) | Ezekiel (1938); Samuelson (1947) |

## Using These Foundations

TER builds on this literature rather than replacing it. These references provide theoretical foundations for mechanisms that TER organizes within its broader architecture — they are not a literature review, and this list is deliberately short rather than exhaustive.

For canonical TER definitions, axioms, formal architecture, claims, and limitations, see [`theory/academic.md`](../theory/academic.md).
