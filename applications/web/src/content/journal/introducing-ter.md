Economic models help us understand decisions, markets, and institutions. They make different assumptions about what people know, what they value, which actions they consider, and how their choices produce outcomes.

The Theory of Economic Relativity (TER) proposes a common framework for expressing these relationships. Built on established economic principles, it aims to make models easier to inspect, compare, and reproduce while preserving their distinct assumptions.

With TER v0.5, we are opening the work to a wider community.

## From decisions to outcomes

TER distinguishes how an agent selects an action from what happens when that action encounters reality. An agent can be an individual, household, business, government, or another decision making entity.

An agent may act on incomplete information, overlook an available option, or attempt something that cannot be realized. Its decision process may involve optimization, habit, limited search, or another mechanism. When valuation is used, actions are evaluated relative to the agent’s objective, model of reality, and time horizon.

Selected actions encounter actual conditions and interact with other agents to produce outcomes. Those outcomes may change the conditions shaping future decisions.

TER gives these relationships explicit definitions and a formal structure. Each model supplies the particular assumptions and mechanisms needed to study its question.

## What you can explore today

TER v0.5 includes a formal theory, an executable simulation framework, interactive lessons, and a documented reproduction of an established economic model.

The [Learn pages](/learn/) connect familiar situations to their TER configurations. Change how options are valued, examine limited search, or explore how beliefs affect decisions. Follow each example from its setup and results to its formal model, assumptions, and implementation.

The reproduction work investigates whether established models can be represented within TER while preserving their assumptions and recovering their results. Its public reproduction page documents scope, progress, verification, and limitations.

The theory and code are publicly available so others can inspect the work, rerun it, and build on it.

## Testing and open questions

TER has undergone extensive internal testing and adversarial review, helping refine definitions, identify implementation problems, and test specific configurations. We now invite independent researchers to examine that work.

Successful reproduction supports a particular mapping and implementation. It does not, by itself, establish the framework’s general validity, empirical accuracy, or originality.

The broader research question is how far this common structure can go: which models it can represent faithfully, whether it makes their assumptions and differences clearer, and where it requires revision.

## Help investigate

You do not need to agree with TER to contribute.

Explore a lesson. Inspect a configuration. Reproduce a result. Identify an unsupported assumption or show where a mapping fails. Careful criticism and negative findings are valuable contributions.

Students, researchers, educators, developers, and independent contributors all have a place in this work. This Journal will document findings, methodological discussions, critiques, and progress.

We believe we have developed something worth investigating. We are inviting you to help determine what it can explain, where it succeeds, and where it falls short.
